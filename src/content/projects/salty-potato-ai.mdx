## Learning the system from the inside

Salty Potato AI is a long-running experimental decoder-only language model that I am building from
scratch. The work includes its own tokenizer, data pipeline, training process, checkpoint
management, inference work, and desktop software around the model.

I started it to understand language models through implementation. Calling a hosted model can be
useful, but it does not force the same questions about tokenization, data shape, training state,
saved checkpoints, or what inference needs to reproduce.

## What “from scratch” means here

It means I am working through the model-facing system instead of treating a remote API as the model.
The tokenizer, data path, training loop, checkpoint lifecycle, and inference behavior are part of
the experiment.

It does not mean every low-level numerical primitive is rewritten without libraries, and it is not a
claim of novel research. The useful part is the practical understanding that comes from connecting
these pieces and seeing where they fail.

## Why the surrounding software is part of the experiment

Training, saved checkpoints, and inference need a clear path through the surrounding software. The
desktop application is part of that learning process: it gives the experiment an interface and makes
the different stages concrete instead of leaving them as unrelated scripts.

## Current boundary

Salty Potato AI is experimental and under development. I do not present it as state-of-the-art,
production-ready, a ChatGPT competitor, or a reason to call myself an AI engineer or researcher. I
do not publish an unverified parameter count, benchmark, or release date.

The project is here as a technical build log and proof of how I learn difficult systems: make the
parts concrete, test the boundaries, and keep improving the product around them.
