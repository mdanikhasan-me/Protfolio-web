## One person responsible for the whole build

I build websites and web applications: business sites, ecommerce products, dashboards, internal
tools, and whatever else the project actually calls for. That covers the frontend, backend,
database, and APIs, along with authentication, payments, and other integrations when they are part
of the job.

The useful part of working this way is continuity. The interface is not designed in isolation from
the data behind it, and backend decisions are not made without considering the person who will use
the result.

## How the work usually goes

We start with what the product needs to do and who it needs to help. From there I map the important
pages, states, data, and technical risks, then build the complete path instead of stopping at a set
of polished screens.

I handle deployment too, and I do not disappear the moment the site goes live. If something breaks
or the product needs another feature later, there is still someone who understands how the pieces
fit together.

There is no rigid ceremony for its own sake. We talk through the real need, I build it, and we
adjust as the product becomes clearer.

## The parts nobody sees still matter

Performance, accessibility, responsive behavior, useful metadata, reliable forms, and clear failure
states are part of the build. They are rarely the first thing someone notices, but they decide
whether the website continues to feel good after the first impression.
